## Overview

Mama Dlo (also Mama D'Leau or Mama Glo) is a folkloric water spirit of the southern Caribbean, especially associated with Trinidad and Tobago, but also with Saint Lucia and Dominica. Her name comes from French Creole 'Maman de l'Eau' — 'Mother of the Water'. She is described as a beautiful woman from the waist up, with long hair that she combs with a golden comb, and a forked tongue; from the waist down her body twists into the coils of a great snake. She functions as a protector and healer of rivers, streams, and the animals that live in them, and is said to punish those who pollute the waters, hunt out of season, or otherwise abuse the natural world. Her tradition draws on West African water-spirit complexes (notably Mami Wata), French Creole and Indigenous Caribbean elements, and she is sometimes paired in oral tradition with the forest guardian Papa Bois.

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Caribbean water spirit** _(Caribbean folk tradition)_

Mama Dlo is a genuine guardian spirit of fresh water, descended from West African Mami Wata traditions and adapted in the Caribbean.

### Skeptical

**Folkloric / didactic figure** _(Folklorists of the Caribbean)_

Mama Dlo is a teaching figure of Trinidadian folklore: a personification used to enforce respect for rivers, fish stocks, and forest ecology, rather than a creature of zoological reality.
